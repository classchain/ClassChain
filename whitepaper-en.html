1. Executive Summary

ClassChain is a decentralized funding protocol built on blockchain technology with a focus on educational equity. Its purpose is to make it easier for Iranians inside and outside the country—and international volunteers—to fund school construction in underserved areas.

Where shortage of educational space still denies children a fair chance to learn, ClassChain provides a transparent, corruption-resistant pathway so donors and young people worldwide can participate directly and with confidence.

ClassChain’s current model is based on direct USDT contributions. No native token is issued for fundraising or speculation. Contributors send USDT and receive an NFT certificate as proof of participation and voting rights.

ClassChain rests on three pillars:

Financial & operational transparency

Via blockchain, smart contracts, and the WebGIS layer

Collective decision-making

Through NFT certificates and a DAO—without a speculative native token

Equal educational opportunity

For children in need; school building as building the future

We believe school construction is more than erecting a building—it is building a durable future. ClassChain is not only a funding platform; it is a social movement in which every participant can matter.

ClassChain is investment in education: a bridge between financial innovation and social justice.

2. Introduction

Education is a fundamental human right and the foundation of sustainable development. A school is not merely a structure; it is a gateway to a brighter future. Yet across many urban and rural areas of Iran, shortage of classroom space and overcrowding still constrain learning opportunities.

Substantial financial and volunteer capacity exists inside and outside the country. What has often blocked participation is lack of transparency, inefficient traditional channels, and costly intermediaries.

ClassChain responds with blockchain, NFTs, and DAO tools to create a transparent and democratic path so any concerned individual can fund school building directly.

On ClassChain, every NFT certificate is a concrete expression of hope, educational justice, and civic participation—not a speculative instrument.

3. The Problem

Educational infrastructure in parts of Iran faces space shortages, aging facilities, and high student density. Despite the valuable work of school-building donor communities, development has remained fragile and overly dependent on a small group of large donors.

3.1 Donor community demographics

An older average age and traditional patterns have made it harder to connect diaspora communities and younger generations in a sustained way.

3.2 Limited mechanisms for small, recurring gifts

Traditional models favor large one-off gifts. Simple paths for modest, regular contributions are scarce, leaving social capacity unused.

3.3 Misalignment with the digital generation

Without digital certificates, transparent voting, and blockchain-based recognition, tech-native participants have weaker incentives to stay engaged.

3.4 Insufficient transparency in spending and reporting

Inconsistent, mostly offline reporting erodes trust and reduces repeat giving.

3.5 Hard access for Iranians abroad

High transfer costs and the absence of a clear channel limit diaspora participation.

3.6 Exposure to sanctions and banking crises

Heavy dependence on domestic banking makes transfers and project continuity fragile under stress.

3.7 Centralized decision-making

Project prioritization is often concentrated; local communities rarely sit at the decision table.

3.8 Funding shortfall

Without creative, transparent collective-funding tools, school-building opportunities remain constrained.

3.9 Missing shared national narrative

Giving for schools often stays personal and fragmented, without a shared cultural story.

3.10 Summary

Blockchain, NFTs, and DAOs can democratize participation and clarify money flows. ClassChain applies that capacity to school building so every contribution and every vote reflects educational justice and public trust.

4. Our Solution

ClassChain builds a transparent digital ecosystem for school funding that:

  • Turns individual acts into collective movement
  • Enables small, gradual USDT contributions
  • Maximizes transparency on-chain and via WebGIS
  • Opens decisions to NFT holders through a DAO
  • Engages younger generations
  • Offers social incentives for smaller donors
  • Empowers the diaspora
  • Uses multi-chain infrastructure less dependent on a single banking path
  • Builds a shared cultural narrative around school construction
Decision power shifts toward the community. NFT certificates represent participation rights and voting power—not speculative trading instruments in the current model.

Each project is either funded directly by larger donors or assembled from many small gifts in a common pool. The community can also take part in decisions such as naming schools.

5. Technical Architecture

5.1 Blockchain infrastructure

Multi-chain design (EVM and TVM): broader access, less single-network dependency, user choice of fees/speed, and support for multiple wallets (e.g. MetaMask, TronLink, WalletConnect).

Core financial logic—USDT tracking, fund creation, NFT issuance, and DAO voting—runs on-chain wherever practical. Contracts deploy first on testnets, then mainnets.

5.2 Smart contracts

  • Direct acceptance of USDT (and supported stablecoins)
  • Independent fund per school project
  • NFT certificates for participation and voting rights
  • DAO voting and allocation mechanisms

NFT standards: ERC-721 or ERC-1155 (and TVM equivalents). Contracts are published as open source and audited before mainnet deployment.

5.3 Frontend

Modern web UI optimized for desktop and mobile; wallet connection; project browsing; USDT contribution and status tracking.

5.4 Backend

Project metadata, notifications, and APIs related to WebGIS. The long-term path reduces server dependence and increases decentralization.

5.5 Fund structure

  • Project-specific funds: dedicated contract/fund per school campaign
  • Common contribution pool: shared resources allocated via DAO rules

Balances are held in USDT and remain publicly observable.

5.6 DAO governance

NFT holders can vote on topics such as selecting projects from the common pool and related development proposals. Voting may be fully on-chain or hybrid depending on cost and UX.

5.7 WebGIS

Interactive maps of project locations, funding status, filters, progress reports, and links to on-chain financial data.

5.8 Real-time transparency

Live dashboards, explorer links, historical trends, and construction/DAO status tracking.

5.9 Security & scalability

Independent audits, backend access control, horizontal scale readiness, and multi-chain support; decentralized metadata storage (e.g. IPFS) planned for later phases.

7. Economic & Funding Model

7.1 Core principles

  • Direct USDT contributions
  • NFTs as participation certificates and voting rights
  • Full on-chain visibility of money flows
  • No native token for fundraising or speculation in the current model

7.2 Key components

Direct funding

Donors send USDT to a project fund or the common pool. A transparent operational share (up to about 5% if applied) may cover running costs. Construction spends come from the same funds. Transactions are reportable via WebGIS and chain explorers.

Two fund models

  • Project-specific funds for direct support of a chosen school
  • Common pool for smaller gifts, later allocated by NFT-holder votes

Role of NFTs

Proof of participation, voting instrument, and social recognition—not a speculative asset in the current design.

Longer-term incentives

Input on location priority and school naming; digital–physical recognition where agreed; opening ceremonies; social standing tied to certificates.

7.3 Financial transparency rules

Smart contracts, progress reports on WebGIS, NFT-holder access to related statements, and DAO-led allocation decisions.

7.4 Economic risk management

USDT focus, multi-chain resilience, avoidance of speculative token design, and contract audits.

7.5 Social impact

Paths for small and large donors and for CSR; human experiences of openings and progress reporting remain central.

7.6 Economic conclusion

Stablecoin for giving, NFTs for proof and voice, DAO for collective decisions—without requiring a native token purchase to “invest.”

8. Roadmap

Vision: a transparent, durable ecosystem for school building with Web3 tools and direct USDT participation.

8.1 Phase 1 — Infrastructure (Q2–Q3 2025)

USDT/NFT model design, white paper, multi-chain contracts, WebGIS design, public contribution site.

8.2 Phase 2 — Early donors (Q4 2025)

Live USDT intake, first project funds, common pool, NFT certificates, early DAO votes.

8.3 Phase 3 — First builds (Q1–Q2 2026)

Construction from raised funds, participation recognition, openings, visual transparency reports.

8.4 Phase 4 — Product & reach (Q3–Q4 2026)

Better mobile UX, stronger DAO tooling, NGO/CSR partnerships, wider chain support.

8.5 Phase 5 — Ecosystem maturity (2027)

Operational stability, more projects, further decentralization. The current model remains USDT + participation NFTs; a native-token secondary market is not a primary goal of this phase.

8.6 Phase 6 — Self-governance & broader adoption (2028+)

Larger DAO role; potential adaptation of the model to similar public-good domains.

8.7 Roadmap priorities

  • Early years: USDT, real construction, transparency
  • Avoid speculative design in the current model
  • NFTs and DAO as the trust and decision layer

9. Governance (DAO)

ClassChain uses a multi-tier model so large and small donors share decision-making. Each relevant NFT equals one vote within its participation domain.

Donor typeHow they giveProject allocationVoting
LargeDirectly to a projectDefined from the startProject NFT votes
SmallCommon poolAfter public voteGeneral DAO via participation NFTs

9.2 Large donors

They fund a specific project with USDT. These projects need not enter the common-pool queue queue. Project NFTs govern naming and project-level decisions.

9.3 Small donors

Contributions enter the common pool. When the pool reaches a defined threshold (for example, enough to fund the costliest proposed project), voting begins. Valid contributions can mint NFTs and voting rights. Allocation follows published rules if a costlier or cheaper project wins.

9.4 Access & incentives

Project NFT holders can access related reports, vote on operational and naming matters, and receive defined social recognition.

9.5 Governance security

Funds follow smart-contract rules after deposit. Voting power is tied to real participation to limit empty voting.

11. Legal Notes & Risks

11.1 Non-speculative, public-benefit intent

ClassChain is oriented toward transparent school funding. The current model relies on direct stablecoin contributions, not a native fundraising token.

11.2 Local regulations

Users must comply with crypto-transfer and charity rules in their jurisdiction. ClassChain does not assume liability for users’ local-law violations.

11.3 Technical & operational risks

  • Smart-contract risk (even after audits)
  • Blockchain network risk (delays, fees, forks)
  • Construction delivery risk across regions
  • Sanctions and international constraints (reduced by multi-chain USDT design, not eliminated)

12. Closing & Invitation

Where educational justice is still out of reach for many children, ClassChain is an attempt to redefine participation, transparency, and solidarity.

Building a school is building the future. That future cannot rest only on scattered gifts; it needs a model where any concerned person, anywhere, can act effectively, transparently, and directly.

  • A contributor is a member of an impact community—not only a payer
  • A gift is a step in collective responsibility—not only a transaction
  • Technology is a new language of trust—not only a tool
This is not only a project. It is a new narrative of trust, justice, and building the future—classroom by classroom.
